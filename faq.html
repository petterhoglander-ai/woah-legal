Woah

Questions, answered.

How the score works, what you can do with Woah, what's free, and how your data is handled.

The basics
What is Woah?

Woah is an iOS app that turns the numbers from your wearable into one clear answer every morning: a recovery score from 0 to 100, and one honest message about what your body needs today. It reads your sleep, heart-rate variability, resting heart rate, breathing rate and activity from Apple Health, and does the interpretation for you.

What problem does Woah solve?

Wearables are great at collecting data and bad at telling you what it means. You wake up to a dashboard of graphs and still don't know: should I train today or rest? Woah replaces the dashboard with a single score, a plain-language explanation of why, and concrete guidance for the day. No jargon, no guesswork.

Who is Woah for?

Anyone who tracks their body and wants to act on the data: people who train, people fixing their sleep, people managing stress, and couples who want to understand each other's days. You need an iPhone (iOS 17 or later) and ideally a wearable that writes sleep to Apple Health.

Which wearables work with Woah?

Any device that syncs to Apple Health: Apple Watch, Garmin, Oura, Whoop, Polar, Fitbit and others. Woah reads from Apple Health, so there are no separate accounts or logins. If a night looks wrong, you can correct sleep, steps or stress manually, and if you have no wearable you can enter values by hand.

The recovery score
How is the recovery score calculated?

The score weighs four overnight signals: sleep is the biggest part (about half), then heart-rate variability compared with your own baseline (about a third), and smaller contributions from resting heart rate and breathing rate. On top of that, life adjusts the result: high self-reported stress, a declining HRV trend, or alcohol and nicotine the evening before can cap how high the score can go, and recent hard training is factored in.

The result lands in one of three zones: Ready (75 and up), Caution (50–74), Recover (below 50).

Why is my score compared to a baseline instead of a fixed target?

Because normal is personal. An HRV of 45 ms can be excellent for one person and low for another. Woah builds a rolling baseline from your own history and scores each morning against it, so the score tells you how you are doing relative to you.

Why doesn't my score change during the day?

By design. Woah locks the score when your night's data first arrives, so it is one stable answer for the day rather than a number that drifts as data trickles in. Tomorrow is a new score.

When does a new day start?

At 04:00, not midnight. Check the app late in the evening or just after midnight and today's score and guidance stay right where they were — you are still living that day. From 04:00 in the morning, Woah starts looking for the new night's sleep.

What is "Today, answered"?

A block on the main screen that answers the day's practical questions directly from your data: Can I train today? How did I sleep? How much sleep am I behind? Should I drink tonight? Am I getting sick? The answers are computed from your numbers, never invented, and each one has a short AI comment behind the info icon that adds colour without ever contradicting the facts.

What is the sleep debt number?

A running total of the sleep you have missed versus your personal sleep need over the last 14 nights, weighted so recent nights matter most. It answers "how far behind am I?" and comes with a 7-day trend so you can see whether you are catching up or sliding.

What is "Tank right now"?

A live estimate of how much energy you have left at this moment, shown at the top of the Today live screen (the news icon on the main screen). You wake up charged by your night — the better the recovery score, the fuller the tank — and it drains through the day: every waking hour costs a little, movement and training cost more, a stressful day speeds the drain, and a nap charges some back. Tap the gauge to see the exact arithmetic behind the number. It is an estimate, not a measurement, and it never affects your recovery score.

What is the Lifestyle status?

Your long-term picture. While the recovery score is about last night, the Lifestyle status looks at your last 30 days across five pillars: sleep, recovery, activity, stress and habits. It is shown as a milestone, from Building to Steady, Balanced, Thriving and Flourishing, so you see the direction of a month, not the noise of a day.

What is my body age?

Your age in years, next to the age your data reads as. Woah takes what your sleep, training, resting heart rate, heart-rate variability, weight and habits say, adds it up in years, and moves your real age by that much. Sleep well and train consistently and your body reads younger than you are; smoke, drink heavily or sleep badly and it reads older. Under the number is a breakdown of exactly which of those moved it and by how much, and a 90-day line showing where it has been. It is free, and it lives on the Lifestyle tab under "Your body, answered."

What does the body age deliberately ignore?

Anything you cannot change. Your family history, any diagnosis you have, and how much company you keep all affect the longevity estimate, but none of them touches the body age — they are real, and they are not statements about how old your body is today. A body age you have no way of moving would be the opposite of the point. What is left is what you do and what your body shows.

Is body age a biological age test?

No. There is no blood sample and no DNA — this is not an epigenetic clock, and Woah never claims to be one. It is transparent arithmetic on the signals your wearable already collects, and every screen it appears on says "an estimate, not science." It also needs real data behind it: if Woah cannot see sleep, activity and at least one heart signal from enough nights, it shows no body age at all rather than guessing.

Why does my body age barely move from day to day?

Because it is built on a 30-day average, and that window shifts a little every morning whether you changed anything or not. Woah caps how fast the number is allowed to move, so a genuinely better fortnight shows up as visible, earned progress while a rolling-window artefact shows up as almost nothing. Answer something about yourself in the improve flow and the number jumps the whole way immediately, because that is a real change and not an artefact.

What is the Longevity estimate?

An estimated lifespan, shown as an age, with the days you have lived, the days that leaves, and how far into it you already are. Under it is a list of the changes that would move it most and what each one is worth in days. The number starts from national life-expectancy figures for your age and sex and is adjusted by what Woah knows about you. You can also reveal an estimated last day, which is off until you switch it on from the card itself.

Is the longevity number a prediction?

No, and it is not medical advice. It is a transparent estimate built on population averages: it says what tends to happen to people whose habits and measurements look like yours, not what will happen to you. The point is not the number's accuracy — it is that it moves when you change something, by an amount you can see and understand. Nobody's date is fixed, and the card recomputes every time anything about you does.

Why can the improve flow make my number go down?

Because answering honestly buys precision, not years. An unanswered question is scored as the population average, so telling Woah that you smoke, or that heart disease runs in your family, can lower the estimate. That is the number getting more true, not worse. The precision dots on the card fill up as you answer.

What you can do
What does Woah do day to day?

Every morning you get your score, the why behind it, and AI guidance for the day. You can log your day (see below) and keep streaks, check the "Today, answered" block before decisions, follow the day on the Today live screen (steps, energy burned, today's workouts and your Tank — how much you have left right now, refreshed straight from Apple Health), get smart notifications (a morning plan, an afternoon check-in, a wind-down nudge at night), browse your history and trends, look back at any past day in the calendar, put your score and today's answers on your home screen with widgets (habits can be logged right from the widget), share a beautiful score card, and talk to your coach.

What can I log about my day?

Daily Habits covers four groups, and every question is optional: how you feel (mood, and whether you are healthy or coming down with something, with symptoms), what you put in (hydration, food, caffeine, alcohol, nicotine, with counts if you want them), what gave back (sauna, cold plunge, open water, time outdoors, stretching or yoga, and whether you had screens in the last hour before bed), and anything else as free text with your own tags. You can log a missed evening up to seven days back, hide questions you don't care about, and tick the everyday ones straight from the home-screen widget. Alcohol and nicotine the evening before affect your recovery score; the rest is there so you and your coach can see what actually moves your body.

Why does Woah ask about screens before bed and not my total screen time?

Because a daily total is a guess, and it is tangled up with the thing it would explain: a late night gives you both more screen time and less sleep, so the two always look connected. The last hour before bed is a question you can answer accurately, and it is the part that touches how fast you fall asleep.

What is the Woah Coach?

A conversation with Woah itself, in its own tab. The coach sees up to a year of your data, helps you set real goals, and builds you a concrete weekly plan every Sunday evening. It checks in mid-week, remembers what you tell it (an injury, a trip, a habit you're breaking), pulls up specific days on request and draws charts right in the chat. Ask it what something does to you, not just how it moved, and it compares groups of days: your weekends against your weekdays, the mornings after training against the mornings after rest, evenings with a drink against evenings without. It only draws a comparison when each side has enough days to mean something, and it shows how many days are behind each bar. It is a wellness coach, not a doctor: it never diagnoses or gives medical advice.

What are the partner features?

You pair with your partner using a 6-character code, and each of you sees how the other slept and how ready their day is, without needing to ask, and without exposing anything you choose to keep private. The Us view adds insights about the two of you together. You control exactly what is shared, can pause sharing at any time, and can pair with more than one person (a partner, a friend, a training buddy).

Pricing
What does Woah cost?

The core of Woah is free, forever: the daily recovery score, the why behind it, daily AI guidance, "Today, answered", habit logging and streaks, smart notifications, share cards, and both your body age and your longevity estimate with everything that moves them. No time limit, no ads.

What does Premium include?

Premium is one subscription that unlocks everything else: History and trends, the Lifestyle status, the AI coach with the weekly plan, the most capable AI model (Anthropic's Claude) for all daily content, the improve flow that sharpens your longevity estimate with what only you know, and the full partner experience: pairing, the Us view and partner notifications.

How much is Premium and is there a trial?

Premium is a monthly or annual subscription (the annual plan is the better deal). New subscribers start with a free trial, and the exact trial length and local price are always shown in the app before anything is charged. Billing goes through your Apple ID, and you can cancel anytime in Settings, under Subscriptions. If you cancel, the free core keeps working.

Do both partners need Premium?

Pairing is a Premium feature, and each person manages their own subscription. The free core is always available to both.

Privacy & data
Where does my health data live?

On your iPhone and in your own iCloud. Woah has no servers of its own and never sells data. Your settings, habits and score history sync to your private iCloud so they survive a new phone, and raw Apple Health data is never uploaded anywhere by Woah.

What does the AI see?

Only what it needs to write your content: your daily values and derived scores, never your name or identity. Coach conversations are stored only on your phone, they are never synced or uploaded beyond the AI call itself. If you use Apple's on-device AI mode, content is generated entirely on the phone.

What does my partner see?

A daily readiness snapshot: score, status and the signals you have chosen to share. You decide what is visible with the sharing mask, and you can pause sharing or unpair whenever you want. Your coach conversations are yours alone; a partner never sees them.

Can my partner see my body age and longevity number?

Only the conclusions, and only if you leave that sharing on. Your partner sees the two ages and the estimated lifespan; they never see the breakdown behind them. That line is deliberate: a nicotine row would tell them you smoke, and the family-history and diagnosis factors would say more still. Turn the sharing off in Settings and the figures are removed from the shared record, not merely hidden.

Who sees the answers I give in the improve flow?

Nobody but you. Family history and diagnoses are the most sensitive things Woah ever asks for, so they stay on your phone and in your own iCloud. They are never sent to the AI, never included in coach conversations, and never part of what a partner receives.

Can I delete my data?

Yes. Delete the app and your local data goes with it; your iCloud copy can be removed from your iCloud settings, and unpairing removes your shared snapshot. The full details are in the privacy policy.

Practical
What do I need for the score to work?

At minimum, sleep data in Apple Health, which usually means wearing your device at night. HRV and resting heart rate make the score meaningfully better. If HRV is missing, Woah asks for a quick 1-to-5 stress rating instead. If nothing has synced yet in the morning, Woah shows a waiting state and guides you through nudging your wearable's app to sync.

A day looks wrong. Can I fix it?

Yes. If the wearable misread a night, you can correct sleep, steps or stress for today or a past day, and Woah recalculates the score, the AI content and your Lifestyle status from the corrected values.

Is Woah on Android?

No. Woah is built on Apple Health and is iPhone-only.
