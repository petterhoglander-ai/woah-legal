Woah

Questions, answered.

How the score works, what you can do with Woah, what's free, and how your data is handled.

The basics
What is Woah?

Woah is an iOS app that turns the numbers from your wearable into one clear answer every morning: a recovery score from 0 to 100, and one honest message about what your body needs today. It reads your sleep, heart-rate variability, resting heart rate, breathing rate and activity from Apple Health, and does the interpretation for you.

What problem does Woah solve?

Wearables are great at collecting data and bad at telling you what it means. You wake up to a dashboard of graphs and still don't know: should I train today or rest? Woah replaces the dashboard with a single score, a plain-language explanation of why, and concrete guidance for the day. No jargon, no guesswork.

Who is Woah for?

Anyone who tracks their body and wants to act on the data: people who train, people fixing their sleep, people managing stress, and couples who want to understand each other's days. You need an iPhone (iOS 17 or later) and ideally a wearable that writes sleep to Apple Health.

Which wearables work with Woah?

Any device that syncs to Apple Health: Apple Watch, Garmin, Oura, Whoop, Polar, Fitbit and others. Woah reads from Apple Health, so there are no separate accounts or logins. If a night looks wrong, you can correct sleep, steps or stress manually, and if you have no wearable you can enter values by hand.

The recovery score
How is the recovery score calculated?

The score weighs four overnight signals: sleep is the biggest part (about half), then heart-rate variability compared with your own baseline (about a third), and smaller contributions from resting heart rate and breathing rate. On top of that, life adjusts the result: high self-reported stress, a declining HRV trend, or alcohol and nicotine the evening before can cap how high the score can go, and recent hard training is factored in. The result lands in one of three zones: Ready (75 and up), Caution (50–74), Recover (below 50).

Why is my score compared to a baseline instead of a fixed target?

Because normal is personal. An HRV of 45 ms can be excellent for one person and low for another. Woah builds a rolling baseline from your own history and scores each morning against it, so the score tells you how you are doing relative to you.

Why doesn't my score change during the day?

By design. Woah locks the score when your night's data first arrives, so it is one stable answer for the day rather than a number that drifts as data trickles in. Tomorrow is a new score.

When does a new day start?

At 04:00, not midnight. Check the app late in the evening or just after midnight and today's score and guidance stay right where they were — you are still living that day. From 04:00 in the morning, Woah starts looking for the new night's sleep.

What is "Today, answered"?

A block on the main screen that answers the day's practical questions directly from your data: Can I train today? How well did I sleep? How much sleep am I behind? Should I drink tonight? Am I getting sick? Tapping the sleep row opens what the night's stars were built from. The answers are computed from your numbers, never invented, and each one has a short AI comment behind the info icon that adds colour without ever contradicting the facts.

What is the sleep score, and why stars?

Every night gets its own grade, shown as one to five stars. It is built from three things about last night — how long you slept, how much deep and REM sleep it held, and how unbroken it was — and then adjusted a little by the weeks around it: whether you keep regular hours, whether your weekend runs on a different clock, and how much sleep you are behind. Stars rather than a number on purpose: the app already shows you a recovery score and a Lifestyle status, and a third number out of a hundred would just look like a rival to both. It is not the recovery score. The stars say how good last night was; the recovery score says how ready your body is today. They often disagree, and when they do, that is worth noticing rather than smoothing over.

What does "Today, sleep" show?

Last night in the order it happened. One bar per stretch of sleep, drawn in the shape your night actually had — light, deep, REM and the times you were awake — with your bed time and wake time at the ends and the totals underneath. It is the same picture your watch recorded, read out rather than summarised, so a night that looks fine as a number but felt broken usually explains itself here. If your tracker only wrote "asleep" without breaking it into stages, Woah says so instead of drawing stages it does not have. A night under two hours is not drawn at all. The night and its stages are free.

What are Routine and Social jetlag?

Sleeping at the same times matters about as much as sleeping enough — regularity is one of the strongest sleep-related predictors of long-term health, and in some studies a better one than duration. Routine shows your typical bed and wake times over the last month, how much they move, and a regularity score out of a hundred, with every night of the month drawn as a dot so you can see when it slipped rather than only that it did. Social jetlag is the gap between when you sleep on weekends and when you sleep on weekdays. A late Friday and Saturday shift your body clock, and Monday morning arrives before it has shifted back — the same feeling as flying two time zones and back every week. Woah compares your weekend nights with your weekday nights and shows the difference in minutes. It says weekend and weekday because that is what it measured; it has no idea when you actually work. Both need a few weeks of nights before they can say anything, and both are part of Premium. The stars and the sleep stages themselves are free.

What is the sleep debt number?

A running total of the sleep you have missed versus your personal sleep need over the last 14 nights, weighted so recent nights matter most. It answers "how far behind am I?" and comes with a 7-day trend so you can see whether you are catching up or sliding.

What is "Heart, answered"?

The stones tell you what your heart did last night. This block tells you whether that is normal for you, and where it has been heading. Today's resting heart rate is shown as a dial. The dial does not fill up from empty like a fuel gauge — it grows out from the top, clockwise when your heart ran faster than your normal and anticlockwise when it ran calmer, with a notch marking your baseline. Length is how far from normal you are; direction is which way. Under it are two fortnight charts, resting heart rate and heart-rate variability, each drawn against your own baseline rather than against a target, and your VO₂max with where it places you for your age. Today's number is free, because it explains a signal you can already see. The two-week charts and VO₂max are part of Premium: what you are right now is free, what only a memory can tell you is paid. VO₂max comes from Apple Watch after a qualifying outdoor walk or run — most people will not have one, and Woah says that plainly rather than leaving an empty space.

What is "Tank right now"?

A live estimate of how much energy you have left at this moment, shown on the main screen just under the day's answers. You wake up charged by your night — the better the recovery score, the fuller the tank — and it drains through the day: every waking hour costs a little, movement and training cost more, a stressful day speeds the drain, and a nap charges some back. Tap the gauge to see the exact arithmetic behind the number. It is an estimate, not a measurement, and it never affects your recovery score.

What is the Lifestyle status?

Your long-term picture. While the recovery score is about last night, the Lifestyle status looks at your last 30 days across five pillars: sleep, recovery, activity, stress and habits. It is shown as a milestone, from Building to Steady, Balanced, Thriving and Flourishing, so you see the direction of a month, not the noise of a day.

What is my fitness age?

Your age in years, next to the age your data reads as. Woah takes what your sleep, training, resting heart rate, heart-rate variability, weight and habits say, adds it up in years, and moves your real age by that much. Sleep well and train consistently and your body reads younger than you are; smoke, drink heavily or sleep badly and it reads older. Under the number is a breakdown of exactly which of those moved it and by how much, and a 90-day line showing where it has been. It is part of Premium, and it lives on the Lifestyle tab under "Your body, answered."

What does the fitness age deliberately ignore?

Anything you cannot change. Your family history, any diagnosis you have, and how much company you keep all affect the longevity estimate, but none of them touches the fitness age — they are real, and they are not statements about how old your body is today. A fitness age you have no way of moving would be the opposite of the point. What is left is what you do and what your body shows.

Is fitness age a biological age test?

No. There is no blood sample and no DNA — this is not an epigenetic clock, and Woah never claims to be one. It is transparent arithmetic on the signals your wearable already collects, and every screen it appears on says "an estimate, not science." It also needs real data behind it: if Woah cannot see sleep, activity and at least one heart signal from enough nights, it shows no fitness age at all rather than guessing.

Why does my fitness age barely move from day to day?

Because it is built on a 30-day average, and that window shifts a little every morning whether you changed anything or not. Woah caps how fast the number is allowed to move, so a genuinely better fortnight shows up as visible, earned progress while a rolling-window artefact shows up as almost nothing. Answer something about yourself in the improve flow and the number jumps the whole way immediately, because that is a real change and not an artefact.

What is the Longevity estimate?

An estimated lifespan, shown as an age, with the days you have lived, the days that leaves, and how far into it you already are. Under it is a list of the changes that would move it most and what each one is worth in days. The number starts from national life-expectancy figures for your age and sex and is adjusted by what Woah knows about you. You can also reveal an estimated last day, which is off until you switch it on from the card itself.

Is the longevity number a prediction?

No, and it is not medical advice. It is a transparent estimate built on population averages: it says what tends to happen to people whose habits and measurements look like yours, not what will happen to you. The point is not the number's accuracy — it is that it moves when you change something, by an amount you can see and understand. Nobody's date is fixed, and the card recomputes every time anything about you does.

Why can the improve flow make my number go down?

Because answering honestly buys precision, not years. An unanswered question is scored as the population average, so telling Woah that you smoke, or that heart disease runs in your family, can lower the estimate. That is the number getting more true, not worse. The precision dots on the card fill up as you answer.

What you can do
What does Woah do day to day?

Every morning you get your score, the why behind it, and AI guidance for the day. You can log your day (see below) and keep streaks, check the "Today, answered" block before decisions, follow the day as it happens (steps, energy burned, today's workouts and your Tank — how much you have left right now, refreshed straight from Apple Health), get smart notifications (a morning plan, an afternoon check-in, a wind-down nudge at night), browse your history and trends, look back at any past day in the calendar, put your score and today's answers on your home screen with widgets or on your wrist with the Apple Watch app (habits can be logged right from the widget), share a beautiful score card, and talk to your coach.

What can I log about my day?

Daily Habits covers four groups, and every question is optional: how you feel (mood, and whether you are healthy or coming down with something, with symptoms), what you put in (hydration, food, caffeine, alcohol, nicotine, with counts if you want them), what gave back (sauna, cold plunge, open water, time outdoors, stretching or yoga, and whether you had screens in the last hour before bed), and anything else as free text with your own tags. You can log a missed evening up to seven days back, hide questions you don't care about, and tick the everyday ones straight from the home-screen widget. Alcohol and nicotine the evening before affect your recovery score; the rest is there so you and your coach can see what actually moves your body.

Why does Woah ask about screens before bed and not my total screen time?

Because a daily total is a guess, and it is tangled up with the thing it would explain: a late night gives you both more screen time and less sleep, so the two always look connected. The last hour before bed is a question you can answer accurately, and it is the part that touches how fast you fall asleep.

What is the Woah Coach?

A conversation with Woah itself, in its own tab. The coach sees up to a year of your data, helps you set real goals, and builds you a concrete weekly plan every Sunday evening. It checks in mid-week, remembers what you tell it (an injury, a trip, a habit you're breaking), pulls up specific days on request and draws charts right in the chat. Ask it what something does to you, not just how it moved, and it compares groups of days: your weekends against your weekdays, the mornings after training against the mornings after rest, evenings with a drink against evenings without. It only draws a comparison when each side has enough days to mean something, and it shows how many days are behind each bar. It is a wellness coach, not a doctor: it never diagnoses or gives medical advice.

The coach is part of Premium, but you do not need to pay to try it: everyone gets five free replies to spend whenever they like, and the app tells you how many are left above the message field. The coach's own check-ins are free to read for everyone — replying is what the five are for.

Is there an Apple Watch app?

Yes, and it comes with the iPhone app — there is nothing extra to buy. On your wrist: your recovery score, "Today, answered", the five signals behind the score with what each one contributed, and tonight's wind-down and bedtime. Subscribers also get their Lifestyle milestone. The watch is a display, not a second brain. It never reads Apple Health itself and never calculates anything — it shows exactly what your iPhone last worked out, which is why the two can never disagree. It follows from that: open Woah on your phone once in the morning and the watch has your day; if the phone has not seen last night yet, the watch says so instead of guessing. Two deliberate details. When your wrist drops and the screen dims, the score keeps its ring and its word but drops the digits, and your answers blur — a precise number about your body should not stay legible to whoever is sitting opposite you. And there is no paywall on the watch: a free user simply has one page fewer, because a purchase belongs on the phone, not at the end of a wrist raise.

What are the partner features?

You pair with your partner using a 6-character code, and each of you sees how the other slept and how ready their day is, without needing to ask, and without exposing anything you choose to keep private. The Us view adds insights about the two of you together. You control exactly what is shared, can pause sharing at any time, and can pair with more than one person (a partner, a friend, a training buddy).

Pricing
What does Woah cost?

The core of Woah is free, forever: the daily recovery score and the start of the why behind it, daily AI guidance, "Today, answered", last night's sleep score and sleep stages, today's live signals, habit logging and streaks, the recent view of every chart on the Trends screen — the last two weeks, with your habit charts free at every range — smart notifications, share cards, your longevity estimate with its day counts, and connecting with a partner. No time limit, no ads.

What does Premium include?

Premium is one subscription for the longer view of your body: the full written reasoning behind today's score (everyone reads the first quarter of it), the Lifestyle status, your fitness age, VO₂max, your last 7 days, the Tank reading of your energy through the day, your sleep rhythm and social jetlag over the month behind last night, the weeks, months and all-time view of every chart, and the action list of what would move your longevity estimate. It also includes the AI coach with the weekly plan, the improve flow that sharpens your longevity estimate with what only you know, and the depth on the partner side: the side-by-side comparison, the Us view, and everything your partner's day contains beyond their score.

How much is Premium and is there a trial?

Premium is a monthly or annual subscription (the annual plan is the better deal). New subscribers start with a free trial, and the exact trial length and local price are always shown in the app before anything is charged. Billing goes through your Apple ID, and you can cancel anytime in Settings, under Subscriptions. If you cancel, the free core keeps working.

Which AI writes my daily content, and does paying change it?

Two things can write it: Anthropic's Claude in the cloud, or Apple's on-device model on your own phone. Paying guarantees Claude for everything daily. Not paying often gets you Claude anyway: the on-device model is only used when the app is in English and your iPhone actually supports Apple Intelligence, so if you read Woah in Swedish, or your phone is older, your daily content comes from Claude whether or not you subscribe. You can also choose the on-device mode yourself in the developer settings, and then nothing at all leaves your phone.

Can I give Woah to someone else?

Yes, if you have Premium or you are a founding member. Your membership comes with a code you can pass on: the friend you send it to gets a week free, then their first year at half price, and the ordinary price after that. You will find it on your own page, low down, and it also lives under Gifts in the ••• menu so you can hand it out whenever you like. There is no limit on who you send it to and nothing is taken from your own subscription. The code is redeemed in the App Store, so the person you send it to does not need to have the app yet — the link takes care of both.

Is there ever a discount if I have not subscribed?

Sometimes. If you have been using Woah for a while without subscribing, the app may offer you a lower price on your first year. It appears on your own page when it applies, it says how long it will be offered, and it is redeemed in the App Store like any other App Store offer. It is not something you can ask for or apply by hand, and there is no code to hunt for — if it is there, it is on your page.

Do both partners need Premium?

No. Pairing is free, and so is seeing each other's daily score, the line under it and the shared "Today, answered" — either of you can do that without paying. Premium adds the depth on top: the side-by-side comparison of your numbers, your partner's full day, and the Us view. Each person manages their own subscription, and one of you having it does not unlock it for the other.

Privacy & data
Where does my health data live?

On your iPhone and in your own iCloud. Woah has no servers of its own and never sells data. Your settings, habits and score history sync to your private iCloud so they survive a new phone, and raw Apple Health data is never uploaded anywhere by Woah.

What does the AI see?

Your daily values and derived scores — and, in your notifications, your first name, so a nudge can address you rather than talk at "you". If you are paired, your partner's first name travels the same way in the notifications about them. No surname, no email, no account: Woah has no logins, and the AI never receives an identifier that could be traced back to you. Your weight and height are never sent — only a band word derived from them. Your family history, diagnoses and how connected you feel are never sent at all; they stay on your phone and in your own iCloud. Coach conversations are stored only on your phone and are never synced, beyond the AI call itself. If you switch to Apple's on-device AI mode, everything is generated on the phone and nothing leaves it.

What does my partner see?

A daily readiness snapshot: score, status and the signals you have chosen to share. You decide what is visible with the sharing mask, and you can pause sharing or unpair whenever you want. Your coach conversations are yours alone; a partner never sees them.

Can my partner see my fitness age and longevity number?

Only the conclusions, and only if you leave that sharing on. Your partner sees the two ages and the estimated lifespan; they never see the breakdown behind them. That line is deliberate: a nicotine row would tell them you smoke, and the family-history and diagnosis factors would say more still. Turn the sharing off in Settings and the figures are removed from the shared record, not merely hidden.

Who sees the answers I give in the improve flow?

Nobody but you. Family history and diagnoses are the most sensitive things Woah ever asks for, so they stay on your phone and in your own iCloud. They are never sent to the AI, never included in coach conversations, and never part of what a partner receives.

Can I delete my data?

Yes. Delete the app and your local data goes with it; your iCloud copy can be removed from your iCloud settings, and unpairing removes your shared snapshot. The full details are in the privacy policy at woah-recovery.app.

Practical
What do I need for the score to work?

At minimum, sleep data in Apple Health, which usually means wearing your device at night. HRV and resting heart rate make the score meaningfully better. If your device does not measure HRV, rate your stress from 1 to 5 in Daily habits and the next morning's score uses that in its place — the rating counts for the day after the one you log it for, the same way alcohol logged in the evening shapes the following morning. Anyone can log it; when HRV was measured, the rating is kept as context rather than used in the score. If nothing has synced yet in the morning, Woah shows a waiting state and guides you through nudging your wearable's app to sync.

A day looks wrong. Can I fix it?

Yes. If the wearable misread a night, you can correct sleep, steps or stress for today or a past day, and Woah recalculates the score, the AI content and your Lifestyle status from the corrected values.

Is Woah on Android?

No. Woah is built on Apple Health and is iPhone-only.

How do I get help?

Email petter@woah-recovery.app. Every message is read and answered, typically within 1–2 business days.

Woah asked me what I think of it. What happens with my answer?

The card that asks “How is Woah doing?” has two doors. “It’s working for me” thanks you, and then iOS may show Apple’s own rating sheet — that sheet is Apple’s, not Woah’s, and Apple decides whether it appears at all. “Something’s off” opens a note straight to Petter; nothing about it is public, and it never becomes an App Store review unless you go and write one yourself.

Neither door is the recommended one, and picking either removes the card for good.

Why did I never get asked what I think of the app?

Woah only asks someone it has actually been working for: at least five mornings with a real sleep or heart-rate source, today’s read finished, and a score outside the recover band. It also stays quiet for a week after the founding welcome, on any day your wearable needed the sync guide, and in any session that showed the paywall.

Someone who has paired a partner, saved a coach goal, shared a card or kept a habit streak gets asked after three mornings instead of five — those are things people do when the app is working.

The card sits quietly in the feed until you do something about it — scrolling past costs nothing and it will still be there tomorrow. Answering removes it for good. Dismissing it puts it away for 45 days, and dismissing it a second time ends it permanently.

Is there a community?

Yes — r/Woah_recovery on Reddit, where people using Woah compare notes, ask questions and see what is being built next. In the app it opens from the ··· menu, next to Product feedback. Feedback sent through the app reaches Petter directly; the community is the room where the other people using it are.
